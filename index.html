A refinancing comes back as a stack of term sheets.

Term sheets arrive in different formats: margin in one clause, the ICR covenant in a schedule, the cash trap in a footnote. If your firm still rebuilds the comparison grid in a spreadsheet, a single misread covenant or fee can reach the client.

Larger firms can buy enterprise platforms for this, such as Leverest, Termgrid and FinLoop. We are testing whether smaller firms want something simpler and cheaper. That is a hypothesis, and we want to hear from you whether it holds.

Compare

Term Sheet Grid

Paste or upload the term sheets received on a deal. Refibench puts them into one side-by-side comparison.

  • Margin, reference rate, all-in cost, fees, term and amortisation.
  • LTV, ICR/DSCR and debt-yield covenants, cash trap, hedging requirement and prepayment terms.
  • The source text behind every extracted value.
  • A ranked grid and a draft memo for adviser review.

The ranking reflects what the term sheets say. It does not claim to know a lender's appetite. The term-sheet reader is a prototype: it flags fields it is unsure of, but it can still misread some layouts, so every value needs checking.

Size

Loan Sizing

Enter or upload income and value. A deterministic engine, written as plain code and never handed to the language model, sizes the loan under LTV, ICR and debt-yield constraints at the reference rate and margin you set.

  • The maximum loan under each constraint, and which one binds.
  • The refinancing gap against the existing balance.
  • A rate stress table.

Maturity view (experimental)

An experimental view of which lenders hold charges over which companies, and when each charge was registered, with an estimated maturity window.

It is shown on synthetic data only. Companies House charges record the lender and the date, not the loan amount, margin or maturity, so they are a weak maturity signal and every window shown is an estimate. It is not used for marketing to property owners.

Built to be checked, not taken on trust.

The output goes in front of your client with your name on it, so every step can be checked.

  • Every figure shows its source

    Each extracted value links back to the term sheet text it came from, so you can check a margin or covenant in one click.

  • Arithmetic is code, not a language model

    Loan sizing runs on a deterministic engine. The same inputs give the same answer every time, and you can follow the working.

  • An adviser signs off

    Refibench informs and prepares. A human adviser reviews and decides before anything reaches a client.

  • Nothing is presented as advice

    The grid, the sizing and the draft memo are analysis for professional review. Refibench does not give financial advice.

Where Refibench is today

A working prototype on synthetic data: every company, lender and figure in it is fictional. There are no customers, pilots or revenue yet. The next step is eight discovery calls with advisers, then three to five pilot firms.

Who we serve: today, small UK commercial property debt advisers; next, as unvalidated hypotheses, borrowers planning their own maturities and Europe; long term, every borrower, adviser and lender in real estate debt, though only the UK has been researched so far.

Later, unvalidated: a sourced knowledge and insights layer for everyone who lends or borrows in real estate: market structure, pricing context, regulation and how refinancing works, with every figure linked to its source.

Why it is being built

Refibench's founder is an analyst in debt and structured finance at a US-headquartered real estate capital markets advisor, working on UK and European refinancings, and has compared 15 to 25 term sheets on a single deal.
